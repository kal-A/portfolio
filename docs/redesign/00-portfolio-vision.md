# Portfolio Vision

**Status:** Design freeze  
**Purpose:** Product requirements document and decision filter  
**Primary audience:** Hiring managers and product, UX, and design leaders  
**Secondary audience:** HCI, UX/UI, information systems, and management technology admissions reviewers and faculty

## Document authority

This file defines the product intent for the redesign. The remaining redesign documents translate that intent into specialized rules for visual language, layout, motion, components, case studies, diagrams, storytelling, and review.

Use this precedence when sources disagree:

1. verified facts about the work, authorship, users, and outcomes;
2. the hiring goal and product principles in this document;
3. the most specific redesign document governing the decision;
4. the current implementation.

The existing site is evidence of current state, not automatic design authority. Preserve working behavior and truthful content, but do not preserve inconsistency merely because it already exists. If two redesign documents genuinely conflict, record the conflict and request a decision rather than inventing a compromise.

## Product definition

This portfolio demonstrates the thoughtful design of products, systems, and decisions through clear reasoning, strong execution, and intentional craft.

It is not a generic PM portfolio, a visual-design gallery, or a personal-brand site. It is an editorial publication documenting how Kamal understands ambiguity, frames problems, makes decisions, works with others, and improves real products.

## Goal

Increase the probability that a qualified reviewer:

1. understands Kamal's value within five seconds;
2. finds credible evidence of product, UX, and systems thinking within two minutes;
3. remembers the portfolio as unusually cohesive and intentional;
4. chooses to continue the conversation through an interview, referral, or admission process.

Employment is the immediate priority. Graduate applications are supported by the same evidence—research literacy, structured reasoning, reflection, and craft—but should not dilute the hiring story.

## The job to be done

When a reviewer has limited time and many candidates, help them quickly answer:

- What kinds of problems can Kamal solve?
- How does he think when the problem is unclear?
- What did he personally contribute?
- What changed because of the work?
- Would I trust him to make thoughtful product decisions with my team?

## Desired impression

The reviewer should leave thinking:

> Kamal has taste, structure, and good judgment. He can turn messy workflows into understandable systems and carry an idea from reasoning to execution.

The work should feel calm, credible, curious, precise, and mature. Confidence comes from evidence and restraint, not claims or spectacle.

## Value proposition

The portfolio differentiates Kamal by combining:

- product judgment and prioritization;
- UX and HCI-informed research;
- systems thinking and diagrammatic clarity;
- implementation awareness;
- editorial storytelling and visual craft;
- honest reflection about constraints, tradeoffs, and lessons.

## Product principles

### 1. Evidence before assertion

Do not ask reviewers to believe a trait. Show the decision, artifact, result, or reflection that lets them infer it.

### 2. Clarity before personality

Personal influences may shape the atmosphere, typography, and composition, but must never obscure employability or the work itself.

### 3. One system, many stories

Every page belongs to the same product. Individual case studies may have a project accent, but typography, spacing, components, motion, and narrative logic remain consistent.

### 4. Purpose before decoration

Every page must answer “Why am I seeing this?” within five seconds. Every image, diagram, animation, section, and paragraph must earn its place.

### 5. Depth without friction

Serve both scanners and deep readers. The main story must remain understandable through headings, captions, metrics, and diagrams; detail should reward continued reading.

### 6. Extensibility over novelty

The system must accommodate Chronicle and future projects without redesigning the homepage or inventing a new case-study template.

## Scope

The redesign governs:

- global design tokens and typography;
- navigation, homepage, project index, case studies, and about/contact surfaces;
- hero imagery and the SVG outline motif;
- page, section, and component layouts;
- motion and state transitions;
- project storytelling, diagrams, captions, metrics, and outcomes;
- responsive, accessibility, performance, and QA standards.

## Non-goals

- Advertising hobbies as portfolio content.
- Building a fantasy, fashion, or cinematic-concept-art site.
- Demonstrating every tool Kamal has used.
- Adding complex WebGL, particles, continuous parallax, or subscription-dependent effects.
- Rebuilding working features simply to use a newer framework.
- Hiding weak stories behind visual polish.
- Treating graduate admissions as a separate visual identity.

## Content hierarchy

The portfolio prioritizes:

1. clear role and value proposition;
2. selected work and credible outcomes;
3. decisions, constraints, and individual contribution;
4. research and systems reasoning;
5. process artifacts that clarify the story;
6. reflection and future direction;
7. biography and personal context.

## Success measures

### Behavioral

- A first-time reviewer can state what Kamal does after viewing the hero.
- A project can be understood at a high level without reading every paragraph.
- At least one complete case study can be scanned in under five minutes.
- Contact and resume actions are visible without interrupting the narrative.

### Quality

- No competing color, spacing, button, or case-study systems remain.
- Homepage and case studies share one recognizable language.
- The visual identity is remembered for cohesion, not a single effect.
- Mobile preserves the story and hierarchy rather than merely stacking desktop content.

### Outcome

- More qualified interview conversations, referrals, and graduate-program interest.
- Reviewers reference specific decisions, artifacts, or outcomes in follow-up conversations.

Analytics may support these measures, but no public metric should claim causality that cannot be defended.

## Decision filter

Before adding or changing anything, answer:

1. Does this help a reviewer understand Kamal's value or evidence?
2. Does it strengthen the editorial, cinematic, and architectural language?
3. Does it conform to the shared system?
4. Is it accessible, performant, and maintainable?
5. Will it still work with five more projects?

If the answer to the first question is no, do not ship it. If any other answer is no, revise it before shipping.

## Definition of done

The redesign is complete only when the system and the stories work together:

- the hero communicates professional value before its visual reveal completes;
- selected work demonstrates distinct, complementary strengths;
- every featured case study supports its claims with defensible evidence;
- shared tokens and components govern every active page;
- keyboard, reduced-motion, compact-screen, and production behavior have been verified;
- a new project can be added without creating a new visual language.

Visual polish alone does not satisfy this definition.

## Success statement

The redesign succeeds when the site feels less like a collection of pages and more like one thoughtfully designed product containing several distinct, credible stories.
