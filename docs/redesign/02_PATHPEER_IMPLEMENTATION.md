# PathPeer - Portfolio Implementation Instructions

## Positioning

Treat PathPeer as a compact Product Management and UX experience page.

The page should demonstrate how Kamal used behavioural evidence and product planning to identify friction, propose features, and create execution plans, with no-code development as part of the delivery toolkit.

Do not inflate PathPeer into a large visual case study. Make it concise, sharp, and credible.

Narrative spine:

> Turning observed user behaviour into prioritized product improvements and practical feature plans.

## Core work to represent

Use only supported evidence for:

- Product management
- Product strategy and planning
- Feature ideation and prioritization
- No-code development
- UX research
- Google Analytics analysis
- Hotjar session-recording review
- Identifying user friction and interaction patterns
- Translating findings into feature recommendations
- Creating PM plans for implementation
- Roadmaps, requirements, or task breakdowns where supported
- Coordination with design, development, or business stakeholders where supported

If video artifacts exist in the repo, inspect them and extract representative stills rather than fabricating screens.

## Suggested page structure

### Hero
Use a concise product-focused headline and one representative video still, UI capture, or abstract reconstructed product-planning visual.

### 01. Finding friction in real behaviour
Explain how Kamal used Google Analytics and Hotjar to move beyond assumptions.

Show a reconstructed insight loop:

`Observe -> Identify friction -> Form hypothesis -> Define feature -> Prioritize -> Measure`

Each stage can open a short synopsis.

### 02. From insight to feature plan
Show how a behaviour pattern became:
- a product problem
- a proposed feature
- requirements or acceptance criteria
- a delivery plan

Use 2 or 3 strong examples if evidence exists.

### 03. Planning and no-code execution
Explain how no-code tools were used to move ideas toward usable product changes or prototypes.

Focus on why no-code was useful:
- faster validation
- lower implementation overhead
- quicker iteration
- clearer demonstration before engineering investment

Do not imply full technical ownership when the work was no-code or collaborative.

### 04. Outcomes
Prioritize product and business value, not vanity numbers.

Potential categories, only when supported:
- reduced time from observation to product decision
- better prioritization of high-friction areas
- faster prototyping or feature validation
- clearer requirements before build
- less rework from using session evidence
- improved engagement, completion, or adoption if analytics proves it

Do not invent financial savings. If a credible time-saved or implementation-cost mechanism can be calculated from a documented baseline, show it transparently.

### 05. Reflection
Keep this short.

Possible themes:
- Session recordings reveal problems aggregate metrics can hide.
- Product research only matters when it changes priority or design.
- No-code is valuable when it reduces the cost of learning before committing engineering time.

## Visual direction

Use the existing PathPeer orange, blue, and white system.

Add:
- subtle orange-to-blue gradients
- pale tinted sections
- strong section numbers
- clean dark borders and offset shadows
- small analytics-inspired visual motifs
- purposeful hover states
- video stills or real UI captures when available

Keep it energetic, product-oriented, and less visually dense than Hera or Greenhouse.

## Evidence rule

If the page lacks a direct artifact for a PM process, use a restrained reconstructed diagram or decision flow and label it as reconstructed.

Never invent screenshots, research quotes, user counts, conversion changes, or shipped features.
